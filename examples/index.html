Why accessibility matters

Around one in six people worldwide live with a significant disability. Many more experience temporary or situational limitations: a broken arm, bright sunlight on a phone screen, or a noisy train. Designing for accessibility means designing for all of these people at once.

Accessible websites are also easier to use for everyone else. Clear headings help people scan, good contrast helps in bright light, and keyboard support helps power users move faster. Search engines benefit from the same structure.

What a widget can and cannot do

A widget lets each visitor adapt the page to their needs: bigger text, calmer colors, fewer distractions. It cannot fix missing alternative text, unlabeled buttons or a broken heading order. Those still need to be fixed in the website itself — the widget is a complement, not a replacement.

Checklist

  • Every image has meaningful alternative text
  • Every form field has a visible label
  • Text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1
  • Everything works with a keyboard alone
